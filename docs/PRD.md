## 1. Product Overview

The portfolio website is a personal digital platform for **Rajdeep Bakliwal**.

It is intended to function as more than an online resume. The website will act as a central hub that brings together Rajdeep's professional identity, engineering capabilities, projects, open-source work, technical experiments, external profiles, documentation, and other meaningful work.

### Core positioning

# 2. Problem Statement

## 2.1 Problem

A resume is intentionally concise. Because of that limitation, it cannot effectively represent the complete breadth and depth of Rajdeep's work.

Important information can be distributed across:

- Resume
- GitHub
- LinkedIn
- Project repositories
- Live applications

## 2.2 Core Problem

The portfolio solves this by becoming the central place that connects, explains, and showcases those different pieces.

# 7. Non-Goals

## 7.1 Not an Online Resume

The website should not simply reproduce the resume with animations.

The portfolio should provide significantly more context and evidence.

## 7.2 Not a Technology Showcase for Its Own Sake

Features should not be added merely because they are technically interesting.

A significant feature should serve the product vision.

## 7.3 Not Overwhelming

The website may contain substantial information, but the visitor experience should remain simple and navigable.

The design should distinguish between:

- Quick overview
- Detailed exploration

## 7.4 Not Completely Dependent on External Platforms

External platforms are important, but the portfolio should remain understandable and useful even if an external platform changes.

## 7.5 Not a Copy of a Reference Website

Reference screenshots and websites are inspiration for:

- Visual direction
- Information architecture
- Content presentation
- Interaction ideas

They are not templates to reproduce.

The final website must establish Rajdeep's own identity.

## 7.6 Not at the Expense of Performance

Visual effects must not make the website unnecessarily slow, heavy, inaccessible, or frustrating.

# 16. Experience

Experience is a dedicated top-level section accessible from the global navigation.

The Experience page should present Rajdeep's professional journey using the same horizontal timeline interaction model as Education.

## 16.1 Experience Timeline

Experience should be presented as a horizontal timeline. Each experience can be selected by the visitor.

## 16.2 Expandable Experience Entries

Each timeline entry should be clickable/tappable. Selecting an entry expands it to reveal additional information without requiring the visitor to leave the Experience page.

The exact visual treatment, animation, layout, and responsive behavior will be defined in Design.md.

## 16.3 Experience Content

Each experience entry should use a variable level of detail.

### Collapsed State
- Role / position
- Company / organization
- Duration
- Location
- Short one-line summary

### Expanded State
- Overview — what the role was about
- What I Worked On — key responsibilities, projects, and work
- Technologies & Tools
- Impact / Achievements — measurable outcomes where available
- Key Contributions — particularly meaningful areas of ownership
- Related Projects — links to relevant portfolio projects
- External Link — company, organization, or relevant work link where appropriate

### Content Depth
- Major/current experience: detailed
- Internship: moderate detail
- Short or older experience: concise

This keeps the timeline readable while allowing important experiences to receive appropriate depth.

## 16.4 Experience and Various Work

There should not be a separate top-level "Various Work" navigation item.

Work directly associated with an experience can be represented within that Experience entry. Independent builds and portfolio-worthy work should generally be represented under Projects.

The Education page should present Rajdeep's academic journey using an interactive horizontal timeline.

Clicking or tapping an education entry should expand it and reveal additional information.


## 17.3 Education Content

An education entry may include:

- Degree / program
- Institution
- Start date
- End date / expected completion
- Relevant academic information
- Achievements, where meaningful
- Relevant links

## 17.4 Certifications

After the education timeline/academic section, the Education page should contain a dedicated **Certifications** section.

Conceptually:

```text
Education
   │
   ├── Academic Timeline
   │
   └── Certifications
          ├── Certificate Card
          ├── Certificate Card
          ├── Certificate Card
          └── ...
```

## 17.5 Certification Cards

Each certification should be represented as a card.

A certification card may contain:

- Certificate name
- Issuing organization / platform
- Completion date, where useful
- Certificate visual or logo
- Relevant short information

## 17.6 Certification Interaction

Selecting/tapping a certification card should redirect the visitor to the **actual certificate**.

The destination can be:

- A verified certificate URL on the issuing platform, or
- A hosted certificate/document such as a Google Drive document, when that is the available source.

The portfolio should not create the impression that it independently verifies a certificate. The external destination should provide the underlying evidence.

Conceptually:

```text
Certification Card
       ↓
   Tap / Click
       ↓
Actual Certificate
       ├── Issuing Platform
       └── Hosted Document / Drive
```

The card interaction should be clear and work without hover.

## Final PRD Decisions — V1.0

The following product-level decisions are locked for Portfolio V1:

1. **Overall Experience**
   - Smooth, purposeful interactions.
   - Animations should support hierarchy and personality, not create unnecessary friction.
   - Respect reduced-motion preferences.
   - Avoid excessive scroll-jacking and unnecessary transitions.

2. **Opening Experience**
   - Portfolio URL → logo/brand animation → Rajdeep name animation → Home.
   - The opening sequence must not unnecessarily delay access to the portfolio.

3. **Loading**
   - Avoid traditional blocking loading screens.
   - Use progressive loading, skeletons, or subtle indicators only when genuinely necessary.

4. **Responsive Experience**
   - Desktop, tablet, and mobile are first-class experiences.
   - Layout may change by device, but core functionality and information access must remain equivalent.
   - Mobile interactions must be touch-friendly.

5. **Recruiter Journey**
   - The primary journey is: Identity → Capabilities → Experience → Projects → Resume/Contact.
   - Important professional information should be discoverable within roughly 30–60 seconds.

6. **Technical Visitor Journey**
   - The primary journey is: Capabilities → Projects → Live Demo → GitHub.
   - Projects should provide evidence of engineering ability rather than relying only on skill claims.
   - Detailed project case studies are future scope for the strongest projects.

7. **Contact Conversion**
   - Contact should support direct email and a simple form.
   - Form success and failure states must be clear and actionable.
   - Basic spam and malicious-input protection should exist without cluttering the user experience.

8. **Content Management**
   - Content should be separated from presentation wherever practical.
   - Projects, experience, education, certifications, links, and resume information should be maintainable without redesigning the UI.

9. **Portfolio Lifecycle**
   - The portfolio is a living professional system that evolves with new work, experience, skills, certifications, contributions, achievements, and links.

10. **Documentation History**
    - Project documentation should preserve previous decisions and updates rather than silently deleting old text.
    - Historical updates should include timestamps.

11. **V1 Scope**
    - V1 includes the locked Home, Education, Experience, Projects, Contact Me, global navigation, responsive behavior, accessibility, SEO, performance, analytics, and security requirements.
    - V2 may include project case studies, blog/technical writing, interactive demos, GitHub/LeetCode integrations, testimonials, events/community sections, and other advanced features.

12. **Acceptance Criteria**
    - V1 is complete only when the required navigation, Education, Experience, Projects, Contact, responsive, accessibility, SEO, performance, security, and functional-link requirements are satisfied.

13. **Core Product Principle**
    - **Show, Don't Just Tell.**
    - The portfolio should prioritize evidence through projects, experience, technical decisions, live products, repositories, certifications, and measurable outcomes.
